# Identifying Joint Pain vs. Muscle Soreness

## Understanding Muscle Soreness (DOMS)
Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (DOMS) is a normal physiological response to challenging physical activity, especially after introducing new exercises or increasing load.

- **Characteristics of DOMS:** Mild to moderate dull ache, muscle stiffness, or tender feeling in the muscle belly (e.g., quadriceps, biceps, chest).
- **Timing:** Typically begins 12 to 24 hours post-workout, peaks around 24 to 48 hours, and fades within 72 hours.
- **Response:** Light movement, walking, gentle stretching, staying hydrated, and getting adequate sleep help ease DOMS. You can generally continue light activity.

## Recognizing Joint and Tendon Injuries
Joint pain or tendon strain indicates mechanical distress or acute tissue injury and should never be ignored or pushed through.

- **Characteristics of Joint/Tendon Pain:** Sharp, stabbing, pinching, or localized pain directly inside a joint (knees, ankles, shoulders, lower back, or hips). It may be accompanied by joint swelling, clicking, catching, or stiffness that impairs normal range of motion.
- **Timing:** Often occurs suddenly during an exercise or persists for more than 72 hours without improvement.
- **Response:** Stop the aggravating exercise immediately. Rest the affected area, apply ice if swollen, and avoid putting heavy loads on the joint. Consult a qualified healthcare professional or physical therapist if pain persists for more than 3–5 days or limits daily mobility.
