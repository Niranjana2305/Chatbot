# Environmental Exercise Safety: Extreme Heat and Cold

## Hot Weather Safety and Heat Illness Prevention
Exercising in high temperatures and high humidity drastically increases thermal stress, dehydration risk, and internal body temperature.

- **Heat Exhaustion Warning Signs:** Excessive sweating, dizziness, lightheadedness, muscle cramps, headache, nausea, elevated heart rate, and pale skin.
- **Action Plan:** Stop exercising immediately, move to a cool or shaded environment, sip cool water or an electrolyte solution, and loosen tight clothing.
- **Heat Stroke Emergency:** If symptoms progress to confusion, loss of consciousness, hot/dry skin, or vomiting, call emergency services immediately—this is a life-threatening medical emergency.
- **Prevention Strategies:** Exercise early in the morning or late in the evening. Drink 16–20 ounces of water 2 hours before hot workouts and consume fluids every 15–20 minutes while exercising.

## Cold Weather Safety Guidelines
Exercising in freezing temperatures requires protecting your airway and skin from frostbite and hypothermia.

- **Layering System:** Wear a moisture-wicking base layer (synthetic/merino wool), an insulating middle layer (fleece), and a wind-resistant/waterproof outer shell. Avoid cotton, as it traps moisture against the skin.
- **Airway Protection:** Cold dry air can trigger exercise-induced bronchospasm (coughing/wheezing). Wear a gaiter or buff over your nose and mouth to warm and humidify air before it enters the lungs.
- **Hydration in Cold:** Cold weather diminishes thirst signals, but fluid loss through sweat and respiration remains high. Continue drinking fluids during cold workouts.
