# Emergency Red Flags, Symptoms, Causes, and Immediate Triage

## 1. Cardiovascular & Respiratory Red Flags
### Chest Pain, Pressure, or Tightness
- **Symptoms**: Squeezing, pressure, fullness, or pain in the center or left side of the chest; radiating pain to the jaw, neck, back, one or both arms (especially the left arm); cold sweats, shortness of breath, or nausea.
- **Possible Causes**: Acute myocardial infarction (heart attack), angina, pericarditis, aortic dissection, or pulmonary embolism.
- **Immediate Action**: STOP all physical activity immediately. Sit down in a comfortable position. Call emergency medical services (911) right away. Do not attempt to drive to the hospital.

### Severe Shortness of Breath (Dyspnea)
- **Symptoms**: Inability to speak in full sentences, gasping for air, wheezing, blue or pale lips/fingernails (cyanosis), or feeling like you are suffocating.
- **Possible Causes**: Exercise-induced bronchospasm/asthma attack, acute heart failure, pulmonary embolism, or pneumothorax.
- **Immediate Action**: Stop exertion, sit upright, use a prescribed rescue inhaler if diagnosed with asthma, and call 911 if breathing does not normalize within 2–3 minutes.

### Dizziness, Syncope (Fainting), and Lightheadedness
- **Symptoms**: Sudden lightheadedness, loss of vision/tunnel vision, loss of balance, or complete loss of consciousness.
- **Possible Causes**: Cardiac arrhythmia, sudden drop in blood pressure (orthostatic hypotension), severe dehydration, hypoglycemia, or valve disease (e.g., aortic stenosis).
- **Immediate Action**: Lie down immediately and elevate legs slightly above heart level to restore blood flow to the brain. Seek urgent or emergency medical evaluation.

## 2. Acute Musculoskeletal & Neurological Emergencies
### Severe Acute Structural Injury
- **Symptoms**: A loud "pop" or "snap" sound/sensation, immediate inability to bear weight, visible joint deformity, rapid severe swelling, or intense, sharp pain.
- **Possible Causes**: Full ligament tears (e.g., ACL tear), complete muscle/tendon ruptures (e.g., Achilles tendon rupture), or bone fractures.
- **Immediate Action**: Immobilize the limb, apply ice, do not attempt to bear weight or stretch the area, and seek immediate urgent care or emergency room treatment.

### Neurological Red Flags
- **Symptoms**: Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the face or body, slurred speech, sudden loss of vision, sudden extreme headache ("thunderclap"), or confusion.
- **Possible Causes**: Stroke, transient ischemic attack (TIA), or intracranial hemorrhage.
- **Immediate Action**: Call 911 immediately (FAST protocol: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911).
