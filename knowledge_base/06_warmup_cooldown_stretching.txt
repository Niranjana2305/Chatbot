# Warmup, Cooldown, and Stretching Safety Guidelines

## Dynamic Warmups vs. Static Stretching
Before beginning any moderate-to-high-intensity cardiovascular or strength workout, it is essential to perform a 5-to-10-minute dynamic warmup. Dynamic warmups involve active, functional movements that increase heart rate, core body temperature, and joint mobility.

- **Recommended Dynamic Warmup Exercises:** Arm circles, leg swings, bodyweight squats, walking lunges, torso twists, and high knees.
- **Why Avoid Static Stretching Before Exercise:** Static stretching (holding a stretch in a single position for more than 15 seconds) before workouts can temporarily reduce muscle power output, diminish explosive strength, and increase the risk of acute ligament strains.

## Post-Workout Cooldown Routines
Never stop intense exercise abruptly. A proper cooldown allows your heart rate and blood pressure to safely transition back to resting levels and prevents blood pooling in the lower extremities, which can cause dizziness or fainting.

- **Cooldown Procedure:** Perform 5 minutes of low-intensity movement (such as a light walk) immediately after finishing your main exercise session.
- **Static Stretching Post-Workout:** Once the body is warm and the workout is complete, perform static stretches. Hold each major muscle group stretch for 20 to 30 seconds without bouncing. Focus on calves, hamstrings, quadriceps, hip flexors, and shoulders to maintain flexibility and reduce post-exercise stiffness.
